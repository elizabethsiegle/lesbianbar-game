Build a comedic 8-bit retro browser RPG called "LAST DITCH: A WESTERN MASS BAR SAGA," deployed on Cloudflare Workers with a global leaderboard powered by Durable Objects.

VISUAL & AUDIO STYLE
- Classic NES/8-bit aesthetic: chunky pixel art, limited 16-color palette, pixel fonts, blocky sprites
- Cozy small-town Western Massachusetts vibe in pixel form: flannel, fall foliage, string lights, co-op bulletin boards, a neon "OPEN" sign
- Dialogue in classic RPG text boxes with letter-by-letter text and a blip sound effect
- Chiptune soundtrack (generated with the Web Audio API): a cozy bar theme, a tense "controversy" theme, a goofy calliope-style clown theme for the Zoom town hall, and an upbeat boss-battle theme for Saturday night
- Retro sound effects for menu moves, selections, level-ups, and meter changes
- Title screen with blinking "PRESS ANY ARROW" and pixel art of the bar exterior

CONTROLS (ARROW KEYS ONLY)
The entire game is playable with only the four arrow keys:
- Movement: arrow keys move the player sprite on a tile grid
- Interacting: walk into an NPC, door, or object to interact with it (bump-to-interact)
- Advancing dialogue: any arrow key advances text boxes
- Menus and choices: UP/DOWN to highlight an option, RIGHT to confirm, LEFT to go back
- Mask editor: UP/DOWN to pick a category, LEFT/RIGHT to cycle options, RIGHT on "DONE" to confirm
- Leaderboard initials: classic arcade entry, UP/DOWN cycles letters, RIGHT moves to the next letter, LEFT goes back
- Show a small on-screen control hint in the corner of every screen (e.g., "↑↓ choose → confirm")
- On touch devices, display an on-screen pixel-art D-pad that maps to the same four directions
- Prevent arrow keys from scrolling the page

PREMISE
The player is the owner of Last Ditch Bar, a beloved, COVID-conscious lesbian bar in a small Western Massachusetts town where everyone cares deeply about rules, process, and community input. Profits are down. The owner considers going mask-optional for one Saturday night, and the town completely loses its mind. The player must survive the fallout and keep the bar alive.

TONE
Affectionate satire in the spirit of Parks and Rec town meetings. The humor comes from escalation: a small policy change snowballs into petitions, a Zoom town hall, rival factions, and a Saturday-night siege. Every faction is lovable and absurd, including the player. Never mock disability, illness, or anyone's genuine health needs. Masks are portrayed as a legitimate choice with real gameplay benefits. All characters, comments, and names are fictional.

HUD
Three pixel-art meters always visible at the top of the screen:
- PROFIT (coin icon)
- COMMUNITY TRUST (heart icon)
- CHAOS (lightning bolt icon)
Every choice visibly shifts the meters with a retro sound effect.

PART 1: STORY MODE (choose your own adventure)
Top-down RPG scenes. The player walks around the bar and town, bumping into characters to trigger dialogue and choices.

CHAPTER 1: THE DECISION
- The pixel bar manager says profits are down and suggests going mask-optional. Choices: YES / NO
  - NO leads to a short "slow decline" branch where the bar struggles in funny ways (the jukebox breaks, only one regular shows up, a raccoon moves in), then loops back to the decision
- "Saturday night seems good. Let's post it!" Choice: POST IT
- The announcement goes live. Show an 8-bit social media feed the player scrolls with UP/DOWN, full of invented comments that escalate from polite concern to 40-paragraph essays, a dog account weighing in, someone posting a spreadsheet, and a person asking if trivia night is still on

CHAPTER 2: THE CONTROVERSY
- "We have to address this. What do we do?" Choices: REINSTATE MASK POLICY / HOLD A TOWN HALL / SAY NOTHING AND HOPE IT BLOWS OVER
- The town hall takes place on Zoom, shown as a pixel-art grid of video tiles, hosted on the only account anyone could find: the local clown school's. Clown students keep joining by accident. Include dialogue choices for handling unmuted chaos, breakout rooms, a moderator in full clown makeup, and someone presenting a slideshow nobody asked for
- Choices determine which factions become allies or enemies on Saturday night

MASK CUSTOMIZATION SCREEN
Before Saturday night, the player designs their mask in a pixel-art editor: base type, colors, patterns (flannel, stripes, polka dots, tiny rainbow), and accessories (patches, sequins, clown nose attachment). Each base type has a STAMINA stat shown as pixel hearts:
- N95: high stamina, reduces "sick day" penalties
- Cloth mask: medium stamina, bonus to community trust
- Novelty mask: low stamina, huge vibe bonus
- No mask: no stamina bonus, slightly faster movement
Masking should feel like a smart strategy, not a punchline.

PART 2: SATURDAY NIGHT (arcade mode)
A fast-paced 8-bit time-management game. The player moves around the bar with the arrow keys and bumps into things to act:
- Bump into waiting patrons to serve drinks before their patience bars run out
- Bump into the DJ booth, light switch, and karaoke machine to keep the VIBE meter up
- Fend off waves of protesters, petitioners, and confused clowns by bumping into power-up stations: free snacks, a heartfelt apology, a strongly worded chalkboard sign, a surprise line dance
- Enemy and ally waves depend on the player's Part 1 choices
- Final "boss": the head of the neighborhood listserv arrives with a clipboard and is defeated through a dialogue battle (UP/DOWN to pick responses, RIGHT to confirm), not combat

SCORING
Final score = Part 2 arcade points + bonuses from the final PROFIT, COMMUNITY TRUST, and CHAOS values (low chaos earns a bonus). Show a tally screen with each bonus counting up in classic arcade style.

ENDINGS
Multiple endings based on the final meter values, each with a pixel-art ending screen:
- The bar thrives with a compromise policy everyone grudgingly accepts
- The bar becomes a clown school annex
- The bar goes viral nationally
- The town forms a subcommittee to study the incident forever
Every ending should feel warm, with the community still intact. After the ending, go to initials entry, then the leaderboard, then credits.

LEADERBOARD (DURABLE OBJECTS)
- Global top-10 high score table styled like a classic arcade cabinet screen
- Players enter 3-letter initials with the arrow keys
- Each entry stores: initials, score, ending achieved, mask type, and timestamp
- Viewable from the title screen (menu option "HIGH SCORES") and after each game
- Filter out offensive initials with a server-side blocklist, replacing them with "???"
- Server-side validation: reject scores outside the game's maximum possible range, reject malformed input, and rate-limit submissions per IP (e.g., 5 per minute)

TECHNICAL ARCHITECTURE
- Cloudflare Worker written in TypeScript (or JavaScript) serving the game and the API
- Serve the frontend (single HTML file with inline CSS and JavaScript, pixel art drawn on canvas) as Workers static assets
- One Durable Object class, "Leaderboard," using SQLite-backed Durable Object storage, with a single named instance ("global") so all players share one board
- API routes handled by the Worker and forwarded to the Durable Object:
  - GET /api/leaderboard → returns the top 10 scores as JSON
  - POST /api/score → validates and submits a score, returns the player's rank
- Frontend handles API failures gracefully: if the leaderboard can't load, show "HIGH SCORES OFFLINE" and let the player keep playing
- Include a complete wrangler.toml (or wrangler.jsonc) with the assets directory, the Durable Object binding, and the migration declaring the Leaderboard class as a SQLite-backed class
- Include a README with step-by-step setup: install Wrangler, run locally with "npx wrangler dev," and deploy with "npx wrangler deploy"

DELIVERABLES
- Worker entry file (src/index.ts)
- Durable Object class (can live in the same file or src/leaderboard.ts)
- Frontend game (public/index.html)
- wrangler.toml / wrangler.jsonc
- package.json
- README.md

Keep the writing short and punchy throughout.
